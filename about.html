About me

The person behind
the systems

HR operations professional, graduate student, and someone who genuinely believes that well-designed systems are one of the most humane things you can build for another person.

My story

I didn't set out to fix broken systems. I just kept finding them.

My career started in counseling administration — a field that teaches you, faster than almost any other, that people's struggles are rarely about the people. They're about the systems around them. The unclear processes. The missing documentation. The onboarding that assumed everyone already knew how things worked.

From there I moved into HR coordination, where I built onboarding workflows, managed BambooHR systems, and coordinated hiring processes. What I found was the same pattern: organizations full of capable, motivated people being held back by systems that were confusing, inconsistent, or just never built in the first place.

Now I work in data operations — and yes, the same instinct applies. Data pipelines break for the same reasons employee onboarding breaks: no one documented the process, no one designed it for the humans who'd actually use it, and no one built in a way to catch the failures.

"I'm not here to optimize for efficiency metrics. I'm here to make sure the person starting their first week, or running their hundredth workflow, actually has what they need to succeed."

I'm pursuing my MS in Organizational Performance & Workplace Learning at Boise State because I want to do this work with more rigor, more depth, and more intentionality. The degree is the formalization of what I've already been practicing.

Systems over symptoms
When something keeps going wrong, I look for the structural cause — not the person to blame. Sustainable fixes come from redesigning the system, not retraining the individual.
People-centered design
Every workflow, training, or process I build is designed for the actual human using it — not the ideal user in a planning document.
Operational clarity
I document things. I create SOPs that outlast the person who wrote them. I build processes that don't require institutional memory to execute.
Evidence over assumption
Whether it's a needs assessment, a data pipeline, or an onboarding audit — I want to know what's actually happening, not what we think is happening.
